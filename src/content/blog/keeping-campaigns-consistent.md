A launch can look like one idea and still fall apart two months later. The film team finds a new grade. The social team crops the mark. The live demo uses a different light. None of those choices are wrong in isolation. Together they undo the system.

Consistency is not sameness. It is the ability to recognize the product in motion, in the room, and in a still frame.

## Hold one idea across every surface

Before the next asset is made, repeat the idea in a sentence. If the campaign cannot say that sentence, it is a new project, not an extension.

The idea might be presence without spectacle. Or a form language that works in a headset and on a flat screen. Write it where the production team can see it: at the top of the shot list, in the stills brief, in the stage deck.

When the environment changes, the idea should not.

## Share the light, not only the logo

Teams often protect the mark and improvise everything else. Light, gesture, and crop do more work than a lockup. If the stills have a cold fill and the film has a warm haze, the brand splits even when the type is correct.

Give production a short lighting note, a crop ratio, and a way people occupy the frame. That is enough to keep a spatial kit intact without freezing every shot.

## Build a loop between campaign and product

Campaigns that never return to the product team become a parallel identity. Schedule a review after the first drop. Look at the film, the stills, and the live demo next to the product UI. Ask what leaked and what should be absorbed into the system.

Sometimes a campaign gesture is strong enough to become a rule. Sometimes it was only right for one week. Decide in the open so the next person does not have to guess.

## Leave a short handbook for later drops

The people who launch the brand are not always the people who extend it. A one-page continuation note — idea, light, type, what not to invent — is more useful than a large archive.

A product with a public life now sits in the object and in the room around it. The next release can grow from the same rules if those rules are written down.

Consistency is a studio habit. It is the meeting after the launch, the still that matches the film, and the demo that does not invent a second brand. That is how the work stays considered once the applause has moved on.
